Restaurant Quality Econometric Modeling

An advanced econometric and statistical study solving the notorious "naive rating fallacy" across Bangalore's restaurant ecosystem through empirical Bayesian rating adjustments, cost-utility efficiency curves, and multivariate regression.

  • Empirical Bayesian Rating Shrinkage: Eliminated small-sample distortion (e.g., a 5.0-star venue with only 2 reviews outranking a 4.6-star venue with 5,000 reviews) using weighted Bayesian priors.
  • Cost-Utility Frontier Analysis: Modeled econometric price-to-quality efficiency frontiers to isolate high-value food destinations from overpriced, low-satisfaction establishments.
  • Multivariate Demographic Regression: Conducted multi-variable regression on location clusters, cuisine categories, online ordering adoption, and booking behavior to quantify drivers of culinary longevity.
🎯 Problem Statement
Standard arithmetic averages in consumer rating aggregators fail to account for variance volatility, causing severe bias toward low-traffic outliers and distorting market signaling.
💡 Econometric Methodology
Formulated Bayesian shrinkage estimators that pull low-sample ratings toward the regional empirical mean while allowing high-confidence samples to preserve their true score distribution.
📊 Dataset Scope
Sanitized and analyzed 40,000+ restaurant records from Bangalore with exhaustive exploratory data analysis and hypothesis testing in Python.
